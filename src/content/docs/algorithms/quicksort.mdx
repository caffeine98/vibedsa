## What it is

Quicksort picks one item, the **pivot**. It rearranges the list into three groups: items smaller than the pivot, items equal to it, and items bigger than it. Then it sorts the smaller and bigger groups the same way, [recursively](../../foundations/recursion/).

There's no merge step. Once the groups are in place, every smaller item is already left of every bigger one.

It's O(n log n) on average and sorts in place.

## How it works

### Partition

Splitting the list into the three groups is called **partitioning**. Here is `[7, 2, 9, 4, 7, 1, 8]` partitioned around the pivot `7`:

The partition does this in one pass, with three pointers:

- Everything before `lt` is smaller than the pivot.
- Everything from `lt` to just before `i` equals the pivot.
- Everything after `gt` is bigger than the pivot.
- Items from `i` to `gt` haven't been checked yet.

Look at `A[i]`. If it's smaller, swap it to `lt`. If it's bigger, swap it to `gt`. If it's equal, leave it. Stop when `i` passes `gt`.

<div class="vd-trace">

| A[i] | action | list after | lt | i | gt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | equal: move on | 7 2 9 4 7 1 8 | 0 | 1 | 6 |
| 2 | smaller: swap to lt | 2 7 9 4 7 1 8 | 1 | 2 | 6 |
| 9 | bigger: swap to gt | 2 7 8 4 7 1 9 | 1 | 2 | 5 |
| 8 | bigger: swap to gt | 2 7 1 4 7 8 9 | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| 1 | smaller: swap to lt | 2 1 7 4 7 8 9 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| 4 | smaller: swap to lt | 2 1 4 7 7 8 9 | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| 7 | equal: move on | 2 1 4 7 7 8 9 | 3 | 5 | 4 |

</div>

After a swap to `gt`, `i` stays put. The item swapped in hasn't been checked yet. You can see this in rows 3 and 4.

### Choosing the pivot

A good pivot splits the list into two similar halves, giving `log n` levels of work. A bad pivot, the smallest or biggest item, splits off nothing. Do that every time and quicksort becomes O(n²).

If you always pick the first or last item, an already sorted list hits that bad case on every split. Picking the pivot **at random** means no input is reliably slow. The chance of bad pivots over and over is tiny.

## Pseudocode

```text title="Pseudocode"
QUICKSORT(A, lo, hi):
    if lo >= hi:
        return
    pivot = A[random index from lo to hi]
    lt, gt = PARTITION(A, lo, hi, pivot)
    QUICKSORT(A, lo, lt - 1)            // smaller group
    QUICKSORT(A, gt + 1, hi)            // bigger group

PARTITION(A, lo, hi, pivot):
    lt = lo
    i = lo
    gt = hi
    while i <= gt:
        if A[i] < pivot:
            swap A[lt] and A[i]
            lt = lt + 1
            i = i + 1
        else if A[i] > pivot:
            swap A[i] and A[gt]
            gt = gt - 1
        else:
            i = i + 1
    return lt, gt
```

## Complexity

| Case | Time | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best, average | O(n log n) | Pivots split the list roughly in half, so about `log n` levels of O(n) partitioning. |
| Worst | O(n²) | Every pivot is the smallest or biggest item. A random pivot makes this extremely unlikely. |

Space is O(log n) on average, for the recursion. It is **not** stable: the long-distance swaps can move an item past an equal one.

## Gotchas

- **A fixed pivot breaks on sorted input.** First-item or last-item pivots make sorted or reversed lists O(n²). The recursion also goes `n` deep, so Python raises `RecursionError` at around 1,000 items.
- **Duplicates need the "equal" group.** The common two-way partition (smaller vs. not smaller) puts equal items in one side. A list of all-equal items then becomes O(n²). The three-way partition here finishes it in one pass.
- **Don't move `i` after swapping with `gt`.** The item that came from `gt` hasn't been checked yet.
- **It isn't stable.** If you need equal items to keep their order, use [merge sort](../merge-sort/).

## In practice

- Python doesn't use quicksort. Its built-in sort is Timsort, a stable merge sort.
- C++'s `std::sort` and many C libraries use quicksort variants. It sorts in place and reads memory mostly in order, so it is often the fastest general sort in practice.
- The same partition step finds the `k`-th smallest item in O(n) on average. Partition, then recurse into only the group that holds position `k`. This is called **quickselect**.
